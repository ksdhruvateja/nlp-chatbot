# Tank Water Heater Troubleshooting

## Safety
Water heaters store water at 120 to 140 degrees F and can cause scalding. Electric units use 240-volt circuits; switch off the breaker before removing any access panel. Gas units have a burner and venting; if you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside. Never plug or cap the temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve.

## No Hot Water - Electric
Check the breaker for the water heater. If the breaker is on, the high-limit reset button above the upper thermostat may have tripped. Turn the breaker off, remove the upper access panel and insulation, and press the red reset button. If it trips repeatedly, a thermostat or heating element has likely failed.

## Not Enough Hot Water
Sediment buildup and a failed lower heating element are the most common causes in electric units. A thermostat set too low or a broken dip tube can also reduce hot water supply.

## Rumbling or Popping Noises
Noise is caused by sediment on the tank bottom. Flush the tank: turn off power or set gas to pilot, close the cold water supply, open a hot tap, attach a hose to the drain valve, and drain several gallons until the water runs clear. Let the water cool first to avoid scalding.

## Leaking T&P Valve
Occasional dripping from the T&P discharge pipe can indicate excessive pressure or temperature. Check that the thermostat is not set above 140 F and that the home has a working expansion tank if it has a closed system. A valve that continues to leak should be replaced.

## Anode Rod
The anode rod protects the tank from corrosion. Inspect every 2 to 3 years; replace when the core wire is exposed or the rod is less than half an inch thick. Rotten-egg smelling hot water can be reduced with an aluminum-zinc anode rod.

## Leaking Tank
Water leaking from the tank body (not fittings) means the tank has corroded through. The heater must be replaced; this usually requires a licensed plumber and a permit.
