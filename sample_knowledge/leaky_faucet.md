# Repairing a Dripping Faucet

## Identify the Faucet Type
Single-handle faucets usually use a cartridge or ball valve; two-handle faucets use compression stems or cartridges. Note the brand, which is often printed on the handle or escutcheon, so you can buy a matching cartridge.

## Before You Start
Close both shutoff valves under the sink and open the faucet to relieve pressure. Put a towel in the sink basin so small parts cannot fall down the drain. Take a photo at each step of disassembly.

## Replacing a Cartridge
1. Pry off the decorative cap and remove the handle screw with a hex key or screwdriver. 2. Lift off the handle and unscrew the retaining nut or remove the retaining clip. 3. Pull the cartridge straight up with pliers, noting its orientation. 4. Take the old cartridge to the store or match it by brand and model. 5. Install the new cartridge in the same orientation, reinstall the clip or nut, handle and cap. 6. Open the shutoff valves slowly and check for leaks.

## Compression Faucets
Dripping from the spout of a compression faucet is usually a worn seat washer. Remove the handle and unscrew the stem with a wrench, replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem and the O-ring, and reinstall. If the valve seat is pitted, use a seat wrench to replace it.

## Leaks Under the Sink
Water under the sink can come from the supply line connections. Tighten compression nuts a quarter turn. Replace braided supply lines older than about 5 years or any line showing bulges or corrosion.
