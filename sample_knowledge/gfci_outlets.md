# GFCI Outlet Troubleshooting

## What a GFCI Does
A ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) outlet cuts power when it detects current leaking to ground, protecting people from electric shock. GFCIs are required in bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoors, laundry areas and basements. One GFCI can protect other standard outlets downstream on the same circuit.

## Outlet Has No Power
If several outlets in a bathroom, garage or outdoors are dead, look for a tripped GFCI on the same circuit, sometimes in another room. Press the RESET button firmly. If it will not reset, check the breaker panel for a tripped breaker by switching it fully off and then on. Do not open the panel cover.

## GFCI Keeps Tripping
Unplug everything from the GFCI and its downstream outlets and press RESET. If it holds, plug devices back in one at a time; the device that causes a trip has a ground fault and should not be used. Moisture in outdoor outlet boxes is a common cause; use an in-use (bubble) cover. If the GFCI trips with nothing plugged in, the outlet may be failing or there may be a wiring fault.

## Testing a GFCI
Press TEST; the RESET button should pop out and power should cut off. Press RESET to restore power. Test GFCIs monthly. A GFCI that does not trip when TEST is pressed must be replaced.

## Replacing a GFCI Outlet
GFCI outlets older than about 10 years commonly fail. 1. Switch off the breaker for the circuit. 2. Confirm the power is off with a non-contact voltage tester on all wires. 3. Remove the cover plate and the mounting screws and pull the outlet out. 4. Label the LINE wires (from the panel) and LOAD wires (to downstream outlets) before disconnecting. 5. Connect LINE wires to the LINE terminals and LOAD wires to the LOAD terminals of the new GFCI, hot (black) to brass and neutral (white) to silver, ground to the green screw. 6. Fold wires into the box, mount the outlet, restore power and press TEST and RESET. If you see aluminum wiring, scorch marks or melted insulation, stop and call a licensed electrician.
